## What the system did well

- **The pipeline's shape held.** Seed board → checkpoint → concepts → editorial choice → generate → edit → review → report. The stage-0 board was the right stop: you picked by ear from playable options, and every later taste decision that went well used the same pattern (the voice board, the voice scout).
- **Memory worked as designed.** Nine Kima notes, each with why and how-to-apply, written at the moment feedback arrived. Reel 2 starts with a real baseline (voice, closing line, logo corners, what was rejected and why).
- **Measurement discipline.** Locked ranges were proven unchanged by picture-and-sound diffs on every export. Cross-correlation found a real 0.74 s audio offset that frame maths had hidden. Four exports with level holes were rejected before you saw them.
- **Tools built under pressure.** The Chladni sand simulation (the Kima mark is plate mode 2,5), prosody transfer (DTW + PSOLA), locked-range diff, source-in-mix locator, datamosh and static generators, SAM-2 matte compositing. 23 scripts in `takes/scripts/` plus `remotion/sand.ts`, none harvested yet.
- **Judgment calls.** Celebrity voice clones declined twice with alternatives offered. Rights on the ripped stock music flagged every time.
- **The records.** The build report and the feedback report are honest, traceable, and already contain most of the lessons.

## What hurt

1. **The agent cannot hear or watch at speed.** Every audio judgment was a measurement or a transcript. Every time its numbers disagreed with your ear (the dip, the drop, the intonation, the conversion artefacts, the closing line) your ear was right. You were the only listener the film ever had, which is why `FINAL-reel.mp4` went back and forth 14 times.
2. **The first cut was over-built.** Remix + pings + chime + sweep + lanes + a photoreal broker. Three review rounds were mostly subtraction. The agent's reading of the atlas ("resolve type early", "near-silence before the reveal") pushed it to fill frame 0 and to automate a gain hole.
3. **Your exact words drifted.** "e devolve" dropped twice, "a" dropped once, a whole line forgotten once. Each cost a round.
4. **One ambiguous sentence cost a production.** "create a completely new reel with that voice" was read as a new concept. A one-line question would have saved 38 minutes and the deletion.
5. **Palmier is shared across runs.** 50 re-opens, "another run's project is active" refusals, an export stalled at 6% for 30 minutes while a second project was open, and the deleted first run's project still counted as active.
6. **Engine rough edges.** The brief is "hey". The 403 appeared as the agent's own message twice and needed a manual "continue". The cost is shown 19× too high. Media spend is invisible. The sandbox refused the zip and returned empty OCR. The run's skill files went stale the moment you migrated Fish and Omni mid-run, so you had to paste an engine path the harness forbids reading. The 2.4 GB reference drop was not gitignored, and deleting it orphaned three clips that the posted timeline itself uses (Tribes, HP028, HP087), so the editable project was broken until it was relinked from the originals on 2026-10-08.
7. **The loop never closed.** The posted reel exists only in a worktree branch. No PR, no ledger line, no insights, no memory routing, no skill-proposal patch, no wiki fold. The engine's whole learning design depends on close, and it was never run.
8. **The atlas rule contradicts how you work.** Production may read only `atlas/patterns/`, which is empty, so you had to override it in the brief, and nine Opus subagents spent 1.34 M tokens re-digesting lenses that a curated pattern tier would have given in one page.
9. **Long replies and slow boards.** Replies were cut off mid-stream; you had to say "give me the html as fast as possible". For taste decisions, a partial board early beats a complete board late.

## Ideas that came from you, not the system

The feedback report lists 21. The ones that generalize beyond Kima and should become rules: picture before the words that name it (0.3–1 s); room before the hook (1–2 s of image alone); pauses at commas, each half on its picture; cut what the picture already says; establish the music bed before the drop and let the arrangement build; build the reveal over the bars and lock it on the hit; lock approved ranges and prove them; audition sound on a playable board before rendering; two-beat sentences are two takes; one base take with restyled middle segments; hard cuts between nameable looks; depth by occlusion; glitch as punctuation on the music; let the picture end without voice; speak the sign-off.
